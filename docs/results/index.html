What we found

The numbers varied a lot, as some slugs had zero nematodes and one had 2,660.

148 slugs total, and about two-thirds of them were Pacific banana slugs

80 slugs (54%) had at least one nematode

Half of the slugs had 3 nematodes or fewer

All slugs together

When we looked at all the slugs together, heavier slugs had a few more nematodes. The connection was weak, but it was statistically significant. Only about a third of the small slugs had any nematodes, while about two-thirds of the medium and large slugs did. Slug length showed the same weak pattern.

Banana slugs only

Then we looked at just the Pacific banana slugs (99 slugs). This time the pattern went away. Small, medium, and large banana slugs all had nematodes about the same amount of the time (61 to 64%), and the big ones didn’t have higher counts. You can see it in Figure 4, where the blue dots go from zero to over 2,000 at pretty much every weight.

Our test results (Spearman correlation between slug weight and nematode count)

All slugs: ρ = 0.25, p = 0.002, a weak positive connection

Banana slugs only: ρ = 0.04, p = 0.67, no connection

Only slugs that had nematodes: ρ = 0.18, p = 0.11, no clear connection
