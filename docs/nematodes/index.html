Nematodes and slugs

What is a nematode?

Nematodes are roundworms. They are really small organisms usually seen under a microscope. They live pretty much everywhere, including soil, water, plants, and even inside of other animals. A lot of them are harmless, while some are parasites and live off of another animal. That animal is called the host.

Nematodes that live in slugs

Slugs can have nematodes more often than you would think. A team in Norway checked 611 wild slugs and found nematodes in about 1 out of every 5. They found five different kinds of nematodes too (Ross et al. 2016). Some slugs get them more than others. Another study done in Norway found that an invasive slug had nematodes more often than the native slugs and snails living near it (Antzée-Hyllseth et al. 2020).

Why do they matter?

Slugs are a big problem for farms and gardens because they eat plants. One nematode, Phasmarhabditis hermaphrodita, kills slugs. Many people buy it and use it to get rid of them (Speiser et al. 2001; Antzée-Hyllseth et al. 2020).

While nematodes usually are not harmful, some are able to hurt humans and animals as well. Rat lungworm lives inside slugs and snails for part of its life. If someone eats an infected slug or snail, it can cause a serious illness (Medeiros et al. 2020).

It is very useful to know which slugs have the most nematodes. It could help predict where these worms will show up most as well as which of these slugs the slug-killing nematodes will work on.
