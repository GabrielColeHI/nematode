# Starter reading list: slug size and nematodes

I only saw abstracts and snippets of these. Read the full papers, confirm what they
found, and rebuild each citation in the BI22xZ format from the article itself.
Only original research counts as a "primary source"; reviews, the Cornell biocontrol
page, and Wikipedia don't. OSU library access should get you the paywalled ones.

## Closest to your question (host size and nematodes)
1. Speiser B, Zaller JG, Neudecker A (2001). Size-specific susceptibility of the pest
   slugs Deroceras reticulatum and Arion lusitanicus to the nematode biocontrol agent
   Phasmarhabditis hermaphrodita. BioControl 46(3):311-320. doi:10.1023/A:1011469730322
   - Lab study weighing slugs of different sizes and exposing them to a slug-parasitic nematode.
     Per the abstract, small A. lusitanicus were strongly affected and large ones almost
     unaffected, while D. reticulatum was affected regardless of weight. Note it tests
     susceptibility, not natural nematode abundance, so use it as context in your conclusion.
2. Antzee-Hyllseth H, Trandem N, Torp T, Haukeland S. Prevalence and parasite load of
   nematodes and trematodes in an invasive slug and its susceptibility to a slug parasitic
   nematode compared to native gastropods. Journal of Invertebrate Pathology.
   (Get year, volume, and pages from the article.)
   - Compares nematode prevalence and number of parasites per slug across species in Norway.
     Good model for how researchers count and compare parasite load.
3. Filipiak A, et al. (2020). Survey of helminths associated with terrestrial slugs
   (invasive Arion vulgaris and native A. ater). Bonn Zoological Bulletin 69(1):11-26.
   doi:10.20363/BZB-2020.69.1.011
   - Field survey of 648 slugs from several countries. Its introduction notes that parasite
     presence can depend on host size, age, and habitat.

## Surveys of nematodes in wild slugs (good for methods and context)
4. "Nematodes and trematodes associated with terrestrial gastropods in Nottingham, England."
   Journal of Helminthology, doi:10.1017/S0022149X22000645 (get authors and year from the article).
5. "A survey of nematodes associated with terrestrial slugs in Norway." Journal of Helminthology
   (Cambridge Core). 611 slugs from 32 sites. (Get authors and year from the article.)

## Related host-size result in a snail
6. A study of helminths in the giant African snail (Achatina/Lissachatina fulica) in Puerto
   Iguazu, Argentina, whose abstract reports a significantly positive relationship between
   host size and parasite measures (CONICET repository record, ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/84024).
   Check that it's a peer-reviewed article before counting it.

## Background (probably not primary sources)
- Ha AD, Howe DK, Colton AJ, Mc Donnell RJ, Denver DR (2025). Microbiome dynamics associated
  with the infection of grey field slugs by the biocontrol nematode Phasmarhabditis
  hermaphrodita. Frontiers in Microbiology. doi:10.3389/fmicb.2025.1619231 (OSU authors; it is
